# How closely this matches Pac-Man Vs.

The rules here were checked against the real game two ways: played and recorded frame by frame from the
player's own copy, and, since October 2026, read from the game's own program with a disassembler and
decompiler and confirmed live with a memory watch (`SPEC.md` has every value and where it was read).
Nothing from the game is in this repository. This file says what was measured and what is still a best guess;
where the program gave an exact value it replaces the earlier measurement and says so.

## Not measured (best guesses in this game)

- The exact frames of the eaten ghosts' eyes on Pac-Man's screen. (His screen's own sounds are now all measured from its sound output: the "wa-ka" for every pellet, power pellets included; a sweep down and up when he takes the fruit, which an earlier reading had put at the round's start; a climbing tone when he eats a ghost; the death sound. Only one eaten ghost was recorded, so whether the tone changes along the chain is not known.)
- How often the announcer speaks (the real game seemed to say much less than it could) and whether some lines are random.

- Points for eating a frightened ghost. Uses 200, 400, 800, 1,600 for consecutive ghosts.
- How long Pac-Man's trail is, and how it ends when he is caught.
- Speed of an eaten ghost's eyes and how long it takes to come back.
- Fruit details: how many pellets trigger it (here 6, then every 70), how long it stays (here 10 s), what Pac-Man scores for it (here 400), how long the wider ghost view lasts (here 10 s; the 1.6 times is measured). I could not capture these in my recordings of the original.
- After the maze is cleared the game here starts a new round with the same Pac-Man.
- Whether a catcher can also win by stealing past the target. Here, any player who reaches the target wins at once.
- Which maze number each menu entry plays. The stage-select screen shows six small maps with names like `DpDay`, `HhDay`, `HhPnch`, `Or`, `UwDay` and `UwEvn`. I matched them to maze numbers by shape and power-pellet count (Original = 1, R.I.P. Park = 4, Manic Manor = 8, Haunted Hall = 13, Frantic Forest = 6, Panic Pier = 5). The links between the six names and these maps are a best match, not read from the game.
- The other seven mazes (2, 3, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12) have no menu entry or picture in the original and may be unused or test layouts. Their names are this project's own.
- Start cells for all mazes except the original. The data holds no start positions. Pac-Man starts in the centre column below the house, the ghosts above it and beside it, the same arrangement as the original.
- The pier stages: the planks are the corridors and the water between them is the wall. Walls here follow the maze data, not the water.
- The CPU players. The original's computer ghosts and Pac-Man are not documented, so these are new.
- How far a ghost can see. The circle here is 4.6 cells in every direction; the original's circle shows more to the north than to the south because of the camera angle.
- 3D lighting. The game sets its lights at run time. The light here was picked so the ghost bodies shade like the original's picture.
- The neon colours. The stage's neon has coloured sections (purple, yellow, green, red) that the game switches on during play. Here the neon stays the scrolling blue that the disc's material shows by default.
- Screen details drawn by the game's code rather than its layouts: the shape of the circles' tabs, the size and
  colour of the score digits in the player panels, and the order in which the results screen shows the ranking and
  then the winner. These were matched by eye to the original's pictures or guessed. (The exchange screen is now
  read from the program; see the row above.)
- The exchange screen's particle effects: the emitters are simulated from the disc's particle file, but the
  program's particle camera is not modelled, so the rays' length was matched to the original's picture, and the
  rays' per-frame flicker in the original is not reproduced.
- The exchange screen's handheld picture and its "take the handheld and press Start" line: the disc's versions show
  and name a particular console, so a plain handheld drawn by this game and its own wording (in the disc's font) stand
  in for them, at the same places, sizes and timings. The original's handheld shows "LOOK AT THE TV SCREEN !" until
  the icon reaches the winner; that picture is not shown here (everyone watches the TV picture anyway).
- The dotted bar next to Pac-Man's score in the player panel and the "COM" labels are not drawn.

## Arrangement levels (not part of the original)

The maze menu's last group holds levels of this project's own, borrowed from the 1996 arcade *Pac-Man Arrangement* (Namco Classic Collection Vol. 2). They are laid over mazes the disc holds without a menu entry (numbers 9 and 7, named here Dash Park and Jump Forest) and over the built-in maze, so the six original stages stay exactly as they are. They are drawn in a look of their own (see "Block world" below). Reference: a full arcade longplay (frames read one by one for the numbers marked measured), Wikipedia's Namco Classic Collection Vol. 2 article, Hardcore Gaming 101, StrategyWiki and the Pac-Man wiki's item table.
