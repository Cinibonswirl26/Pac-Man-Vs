# How closely this matches Pac-Man Vs.

The rules here were checked against the real game, played and recorded frame by frame from the
player's own copy. Nothing from the game is in this repository. This file says what was measured
and what is still a best guess.

## Measured from the original

| Rule | Value | How it was checked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maze (stage "Original Pac-Man") | 19 x 21 cells, mirror-symmetric, one tunnel row (row 10), ghost house in the middle: a 3 x 1 room with walls on both sides and below, and a gate of two wall pieces with a one-cell door between them (checked against the stage model; only eaten ghosts pass the door freely) | Tile map stored in the game's data, compared with the in-game picture |
| Pellets | 172 small + 4 power = **176**. Power pellets at (0,2), (18,2), (0,16), (18,16) | Counted in the tile map, matches the on-screen counter at the start of a round |
| Start cells | Pac-Man (9,12); ghosts (6,6), (12,6), (9,8) | Sprite positions at round start |
| Pac-Man speed | **0.5 px per frame** = 3.73 cells per second on a cleared corridor; **eating a pellet holds him for one frame**, so over a row of pellets he averages 0.4725 px per frame | Sprite tracked over hundreds of frames; controlled run along the bottom row: 0.4725 px/frame eating 16 pellets, 0.503 back over the cleared row (the difference is 1 frame per pellet) |
| Warp tunnel | The tunnel row carries on out of sight: from the centre of the last cell on one side to the centre of the last cell on the other is about 3.3 cells, 2.3 of them hidden. Every ghost (grey stand-ins too) moves at **three quarters speed** (0.375 px/frame) within the outer 2.5 cells of the row and through the hidden stretch; Pac-Man is not slowed | Controlled run in the emulator: a ghost driven along the tunnel row read 0.504 px/frame in the open and 0.374-0.391 in the outer two cells, and took 70-71 frames from the last cell's centre on one side to the last cell's centre on the other, both ways. Pac-Man's own crossing was not timed (the handheld's picture clips him at the edge); his speed is assumed unchanged |
| Turning | Pac-Man turns at the cell centre: with the new direction held early he carries on to the centre, then moves off at right angles (no corner cutting) | Controlled run: UP held from 0.4 cell before a junction; x stayed on the junction's column while y changed |
| Ghost speed | **about 0.504 px per frame**, a slow gain of roughly 1% on Pac-Man (1 px per ~128 px run) | Pixel steps of ghosts on long straight runs: 2 frames per pixel with an extra 1-frame step every ~80 px, never a 3-frame step; Pac-Man's steps average exactly 2.00 frames. About 570 ghost steps, so the size of the gain is an estimate (0.3 to 0.8%) |
| Frightened ghost speed | **0.375 px per frame** (3/4 of normal) = 2.80 cells per second | Straight runs during the effect; controlled run in the emulator: the same ghost driven along the bottom row read 0.502 px/frame before the power pellet and 0.378-0.383 px/frame while blue (two runs, 16 and 9 cells) |
| Frightened time | **10 s** (598 frames). Last 2 s the ghosts blink every 8 frames | Sprite tile changes, frame by frame |
| Pellet | 10 points | Score before and after |
| Power pellet | 110 points | Score before and after, pellet counter 167 -> 166 |
| Player colours | P1 red, P2 blue, P3 pink, P4 green | In-game screens |
| Goal | A target score is chosen before the match (7,000 shown first) | "Choose a score" screen |
| First Pac-Man | Picked by a roulette. Starts with **1,600** points | Scoreboard at round start, three separate matches |
| Catching Pac-Man | The round ends at once. The catcher **steals 1,600** points from Pac-Man (he keeps the rest, e.g. 1,610 -> 10) and **becomes the next Pac-Man** | Loser/winner screen: -1,600 / +1,600 |
| Hand-over | After the exchange screen the next round waits for the new Pac-Man to press Start | The exchange screen asks the new Pac-Man to press Start, with an arrow at them. Here the new Pac-Man presses a key or Ready; the 15-second limit is this game's own |
| Round reset | Pellets back to 176, everyone back to their start cells | Next round |
| Round timer | None found. A round ran over 80 s with no clock | HUD and play |
| Ghost view | A circle around each ghost, a minimap of the ghosts and a pellet counter. All ghost players watch the same screen, so each sees every ghost's circle | shared screen |
| Fruit (from published descriptions, plus one 400-point score seen on a ghost) | Appears in the middle of the maze once enough pellets are gone. Pac-Man or a ghost can take it. A ghost gets 400 points and a wider view for a while | Wikipedia and reviews; a ghost's 400 points seen in the emulator |
| Clearing the maze | Pac-Man gets a 1,600 bonus | Wikipedia |
| Stages | The disc holds 13 mazes, one for each of its 13 stage models, in the same order. Walls and pellets are read from the maze data; on every model the pellet cells sit on a walkable surface at one height | `test/mazedata.test.js`, `test/j3d.test.js` (the 3D models drawn over the maze data) |
| Ghost blink | The eyes cycle through five textures once every 64 frames (open, half, closed, half, open) | The disc's texture-pattern animation |
| Ghost view camera | Looks north, 38 degrees down; a circle 252 px across on the 640 x 480 picture shows about 5.7 cells at the ghost | Rendered from the same cell and compared with the original's picture; pellet rows and wall edges line up |
| shared screen layout | The disc's `pac_game.blo` scaled by 640/608 in both directions and moved 2 px down; circles 252 px across centred at (145, 146), (495, 146) and (319, 330). Each circle belongs to a ghost slot, not a player: the slots start in seat order and a player keeps theirs from round to round; after a catch the old Pac-Man takes over the catcher's slot (in the original, after P1 caught P3, P3 was in P1's old top-left circle with P2 still top-right) | Fitted against the original's picture: panels, counter, radar and score line up; circle centres and frame colours measured; the slot hand-over seen across one catch in a three-player game |
| Exchange screen | Four circles 140 px across, 152 px apart, LOSER / WINNER above, score bars and the points moved below. A stand-in's circle is grey with the grey ghost and "COM" and has no score bar | Measured on the original's picture after a catch; the COM circle seen in the three-player game |
| Results screen | The disc's layout has four-, three- and two-player arrangements; the one for the number of people playing is used, stand-ins left out | Disc layout (`pac_results.blo`); the original's three-player results were not recorded |
| Fruit pickup zoom | When a ghost takes the fruit its circle's picture pulls back to about 1.6 times the reach (by the measured area of the ghost in the circle), easing out over about 1.1 s (67 frames of the 59.7 Hz clock), with a "400" floating over the ghost and white sparkles for about 0.3 s. It stayed zoomed for the 8 s I recorded | Frame by frame in the emulator |
| Frightened look | Scene dims; ghosts turn dark blue with a wavy mouth and no big eyes, the body the same size as before (measured in the emulator on one ghost standing still through a power pellet: 33 px wide red, 31-33 px wide blue, same top edge; an earlier reading had them smaller); cyan sparkles trail them; in the last 2 s they blink out; at the end pink rings burst and the ghost returns | Frame by frame in the emulator (blink timing roughly 0.15 s on and off) |
| Pac-Man's trail | A wide flat ribbon on the floor (nearly a full corridor wide) in the Pac-Man player's colour, near-opaque, paler next to Pac-Man and fading at its tail; ghosts see it inside their circles | Seen in the emulator in all circles; the length is a guess (2 s here) |
| Catch sequence | The moment Pac-Man is caught, yellow rays burst round him in every circle that shows him, a grey "1600" floats up from the spot, and he is knocked flat. About 2 s later the whole picture fades to amber, then a black box with an orange border says EXCELLENT next to the catcher's icon and player number (the disc's own `pac_game_mes` layout). About 7 s after the catch it fades to black and the exchange screen follows | Frame by frame in the emulator, one catch; timings are from snapshots about 1 s of game time apart |
| Score popups and fruit stars | Points (400 for the fruit, 200+ for a ghost, 1,600 for a catch) float up in grey over whoever scored. When the fruit goes, taken or timed out, white stars burst out of the fruit window across the picture for under a second | Frame by frame in the emulator; the popup look is from the ghost-eaten and catch frames, the 400 is the same effect |
| Pac-Man's screen | Drawn from the disc's handheld program: the maze and panel tiles, the colour tables, the ghosts (four colours, four directions, two feet frames), the frightened ghosts with their player-number badges, eyes, cherry and score digits. The picture was overlaid on recordings of the real screen: tiles, panel, sprites and colours matched pixel for pixel. The camera keeps Pac-Man's sprite between 36 and 88 px from the top and never stops at the maze's edge, so the black below the maze shows | Overlaid on recorded frames (the only differences were eaten pellets and score digits, which the game state supplies) |
| Pac-Man's animation | Mouth: closed, then three open phases, two pictures each (a cycle every 8 pictures while moving); ghosts' feet swap every 12 pictures; frightened ghosts alternate two faces; "Press START!" blinks 32 pictures on, 32 off; score pop-ups use small digits 4 px apart | Sprite lists recorded over thousands of frames |
| Pac-Man's sounds | "Wa-ka" for every pellet (six rising notes, a two-picture rest, six falling, one note per picture, 12.5% pulse wave) and the sweep that starts a round (440 Hz down to 130, up to 500, fading, two tones an octave apart) | Pitch, timing, loudness and wave shape measured from recordings of the handheld's output |
| Announcer | 36 voice lines and 16 effects are read from the disc's wave banks. What each line says was found by speech recognition run on the lines themselves ("Ready", "Go", "Fruit appeared", "Pac-Man ate the fruit", "[colour] got the fruit", "Excellent, [colour]", "[colour] is the champion", "Only ten pellets left", "One thousand / five hundred points left", ...) | Decoded from the disc |
| Which sound plays when | Matched against recordings of the original while Pac-Man cleared a maze: Ready and Go at the start; "Oh no" lines 10, 11, 12 for the first, second and third ghost eaten after one power pellet; "Only ten pellets left" (18) at ten pellets left; "This doesn't look good" (20) at about twenty left; the maze-cleared line (17) when the last pellet goes; "End of the game" (30) when someone reaches the target; "Title" (35) when the game boots; fruit chime with "Fruit appeared". After power pellets the announcer spoke after only some of them (3 of 5 here): line 1 twice and "You can do it!" (34) once, so the power-pellet lines play with 60% chance here. Not seen at all: 21 and the points-left lines (28, 29) in these runs. "Go go go!" (33) and "You can do it!" (34) are on a timer by choice: one minute and two and a half minutes into a round, starting again each round (I could not find what triggers them in the original) Effects matched the same way: pellet eaten (9) on every pellet, power pellet (8), the frightened-ghost loop (10) for as long as the ghosts are blue, the two-tone "wee-woo" siren (12) looping while an eaten ghost flies home, and the rising sweep with a thump (11 and 13) when a ghost is eaten | Recorded and matched by waveform
| Music (Original stage) | Title and menus: the opening theme stream (`Pac_Opening_Comp`), looping. Each round's countdown: the classic start jingle stream (`pacmanBGM00`, 4.6 s), every round. While the round runs: no music, a siren sequenced by the game's synthesiser: a square-like tone (odd harmonics) held at 563 Hz for about 0.12 s, rising to 844 Hz over about 0.18 s and falling back over about 0.13 s, every 0.43 s; it did not change over the 50 s measured (few pellets eaten). After a catch: about 6 s of silence (the knock and the message), then a one-voice looping tune (4 s: C7 C7 C7 A6 G6 C7 D#7 E7 E7 E7 D#7 C#7 C7 C7 C7 A6 G6 C7 A6 G#6 G6) through the exchange and hand-over screens, also sequenced. Both are synthesised here from these measurements. The pacmania streams were not heard on this stage | The emulator's sound output over three rounds matched against every stream on the disc (spectrogram correlation, 0.97 for the jingle, 0.38 for the opening theme under the menu effects, nothing for the pacmania tracks); the siren's pitch tracked at 5 ms steps; the tune's notes read off its spectrogram. Not yet checked: the other stages (probably the pacmania streams), the results screen, whether the siren changes as pellets go |
| Original stage floor | Plain dark floor with the walls' blue neon edges: the floor texture sheet is held at its edge instead of repeating, because repeating it printed grey wall-edge art on the floor that did not line up with the walls (it did not appear in the original's picture) | Disc model; compared with the original's recorded picture |
| Frightened ghosts' drops | Small glowing blue drops fly off a frightened ghost while the effect lasts: about 4 px wide on the 640 px picture (0.09 cell), each alive for about 6 frames (0.2 s), two or three in the air per ghost, leaving from where the ghost was and flying out sideways in any direction at about 1.4 cells/s and upwards at about 0.75 cells/s, shrinking at the end; so a moving ghost leaves a short trail of them behind it | Tracked frame by frame in a 30 fps recording of the original (207 frames): 164 drop tracks, median life 5 frames, speed 4.3 px/frame on the recording's 1.48x picture |
| Grey stand-in ghosts | An empty ghost seat is a grey ghost (the disc's own grey "com" ghost skin): Pac-Man passes through it and it cannot catch him. It wanders at about 0.33 px a frame, two thirds of ghost speed (the ghosts 0.504, Pac-Man 0.5), so a ghost can run into it on purpose; on Pac-Man's handheld it is drawn as a pair of eyes only, like an eaten ghost, until it wakes. A ghost player passing through it wakes it up in that player's colour; it then moves at ghost speed and goes straight for Pac-Man, and if it catches him that player takes the points and is Pac-Man next. If Pac-Man eats it while it is frightened it turns grey again. Grey ghosts are not frightened by power pellets and do not take the fruit. Each empty seat is either a stand-in or a computer player (chosen per seat in the menu, and by the host in the lobby); Pac-Man never starts as a stand-in while there are two or more players. A computer player keeps the CPU ghost AI | Observed in the original in three-player mode: grey circle, panel row, wandering, wake-up. Measured in the emulator over three rounds: the grey ghost's square on the radar moved at 0.30 px/frame by the same method that gave the woken ghost 0.44 (its true 0.48 was read off the handheld), so about 0.33; once woken (it ran into an idle player's ghost both times) it moved at 0.48-0.50 px/frame on the handheld and went for Pac-Man, catching him within seconds, the catch credited to the player whose colour it wore. Not seen: Pac-Man walking through a grey one. On the handheld it was a pair of eyes while grey (frames saved at a round's start), a coloured body once woken |
| Stand-ins on the screens | The shared screen's panel lists the people playing in seat order with the last of them on the bottom row and the stand-ins between (with three players: P1, P2, COM, P3), the stand-in's row grey with the grey ghost icon and the layout's own COM mark instead of a score and number. Its circle has a grey frame and "COM" in the tab. The exchange screen keeps the same order | Original in three-player mode: panel rows, circle, exchange and results-after-catch screens; the layout's `P2_COM` and `P3_COM` panes are the COM marks for rows 2 and 3 |
| Ghost body | Disc model with its 64-frame float animation played at 30 steps a second (half the 60 Hz frame clock, as the blink is): the body bobs once every 128 frames and the skirt sways twice in that time, the player's skin (ghost_r/b/p/g), the frightened skin (ghost_yowari) and an invisible body for eaten ghosts (ghost_toumei) | Measured in the emulator on an idle ghost (40 s recording, timed against the handheld's frame counter): bob period 127 frames, skirt period 64 frames, bob about 1.7 px on a 33 px body; ours at this rate: 130 and 65 frames, 1.4 px on 32 px |

## Not measured (best guesses in this game)

- Pac-Man's own sounds for eating a ghost, taking the fruit and being caught (the handheld stays silent for them here; I could not trigger them in my recordings), and the exact frames of the Pac-Man death animation and the eaten ghosts' eyes on his screen.
- How often the announcer speaks (the real game seemed to say much less than it could) and whether some lines are random.

- Points for eating a frightened ghost. Uses 200, 400, 800, 1,600 for consecutive ghosts.
- How long Pac-Man's trail is, and how it ends when he is caught.
- Speed of an eaten ghost's eyes and how long it takes to come back.
- Fruit details: how many pellets trigger it (here 6, then every 70), how long it stays (here 10 s), what Pac-Man scores for it (here 400), how long the wider ghost view lasts (here 10 s; the 1.6 times is measured). I could not capture these in my recordings of the original.
- After the maze is cleared the game here starts a new round with the same Pac-Man.
- Whether a catcher can also win by stealing past the target. Here, any player who reaches the target wins at once.
- Which maze number each menu entry plays. The stage-select screen shows six small maps with names like `DpDay`, `HhDay`, `HhPnch`, `Or`, `UwDay` and `UwEvn`. I matched them to maze numbers by shape and power-pellet count (Original = 1, R.I.P. Park = 4, Manic Manor = 8, Haunted Hall = 13, Frantic Forest = 6, Panic Pier = 5). The links between the six names and these maps are a best match, not read from the game.
- The other seven mazes (2, 3, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12) have no menu entry or picture in the original and may be unused or test layouts. Their names are this project's own.
- Start cells for all mazes except the original. The data holds no start positions. Pac-Man starts in the centre column below the house, the ghosts above it and beside it, the same arrangement as the original.
- The pier stages: the planks are the corridors and the water between them is the wall. Walls here follow the maze data, not the water.
- The CPU players. The original's computer ghosts and Pac-Man are not documented, so these are new.
- How far a ghost can see. The circle here is 4.6 cells in every direction; the original's circle shows more to the north than to the south because of the camera angle.
- 3D lighting. The game sets its lights at run time. The light here was picked so the ghost bodies shade like the original's picture.
- The neon colours. The stage's neon has coloured sections (purple, yellow, green, red) that the game switches on during play. Here the neon stays the scrolling blue that the disc's material shows by default.
- Screen details drawn by the game's code rather than its layouts: the shape of the circles' tabs, the size and
  colour of the score digits in the player panels, how the exchange screen animates and when its arrow appears,
  and the order in which the results screen shows the ranking and then the winner. These were matched by eye
  to the original's pictures or guessed.
- The dotted bar next to Pac-Man's score in the player panel and the "COM" labels are not drawn.
